P18 supplier behaviour — evidence review, 25 October 2026

Conclusion
The supplier recognises our relevant evidence. The remaining failure is that
the complete package it offers does not clear our chosen acceptance threshold.
Evidence verification, acceptance of individual terms, and agreement on the
whole package are three different events. The strategy must track them separately.

Scope and reproducibility
30 saved hosted P18 runs, plus 30 fresh read-only GET snapshots of their owned
sessions. All 30 sessions are ended. There were 10 accepted deals, 15 supplier
withdrawals and 5 buyer API closures. The five closures must not be counted as
supplier reactions. This review started no session and made no paid model call.

observations.json contains the calculated results and links to each original run.
sessions/ contains the server snapshots, including the actual interpreted claim
groups and their results. scripts/analyze_p18_behavior.py rebuilds the analysis
offline. It excludes repeated local acceptance confirmations from offer counts.
It checks session identity, claim/result mapping and uniqueness of offer IDs.

This is an observational sample, not a randomised experiment. Strategies, prices,
payment terms, duration and clause changed between runs. We can report what
followed a message and what the supplier said; we cannot assign an exact causal
price discount to each argument or infer an undisclosed reservation price.

3. Observed commercial behaviour

Price increments:
  Every one of the 112 quoted packages after the opening used EUR 0.10 increments.
  This includes primary quotes and advertised alternatives, not duplicate order
  confirmations. None quoted EUR 5.05, 5.07 or 5.09. The lowest observed price was
  EUR 5.10. This is a strong planning observation, not a documented price-grid
  restriction or proof that EUR 5.00 or EUR 5.09 is impossible. Never round a live
  offer or refuse a valid qualifying offer merely because it breaks this pattern.

Payment and clause are priced together:
  The latest menu priced 60 days at EUR 5.40 and 30 days at EUR 5.30, with the
  same clause/duration. Longer credit is not a free concession. In other runs,
  a 5% deadband alternative cost EUR 0.10 more than a 0% deadband package.
  Neither surcharge is proven universal. It explains why winning a nominal
  price while later asking for more credit or a different clause can fail.

Partial acceptance:
  Phrases such as "we accept your proposed 45-day payment term" do not mean
  "we accept your offer". Read the complete structured term sheet and its live
  status. In cfed77f5... narrative and structured primary price even differed;
  use protocol eligibility and exact quoted terms for acceptance, not sentiment.

Authority:
  Latest message 3 says Seidl is fully authorised. In 54747c61..., the supplier
  said the committee had reviewed and authorised its final price. Repeating an
  escalation request is not a demonstrated source of another concession.
  Historical approval records justify asking early if necessary; once answered,
  record the answer. Current supplier_signals.py matches "authoriz" inside its
  approval_required cue even when authority is confirmed. That cue is only a
  language signal and must not be interpreted as proof of missing approval.

Finality:
  26 runs reached a final offer: 9 after the second buyer proposal, 14 after the
  third, 3 after the fourth. Every first final offer had a 7-day validity signal.
  Of those runs, 15 sent a further email: all 15 received supplier withdrawal.
  Eight accepted the final offer; three closed via the buyer API. Thus the
  demonstrated successful response to an acceptable final offer is acceptance.
  Do not treat remaining API rounds, a polite farewell, a volume question, or
  later "valid for 14 days" boilerplate as permission to reopen a terminal state.

Tone:
  Polite language appears in concessions, final offers and withdrawals. Warmth
  is appropriate, but the records do not establish a sentiment-based discount.
  Commercial stance and exact terms should drive decisions, not cordial words.

5. Proposed next controlled structure — hypothesis, not activated
The next useful question is whether EUR 5.10/60 days/5% deadband can be reached
when that structure is established from the outset. No saved opening included
60 days with the 5% clause. It has appeared later as a menu item, at higher prices.
This is therefore a distinct test of package consistency, not proof of a new lever.

Hold the model, polite tone, authorised scope and PO -> PPI -> AS sequence fixed.
Use a single complete package path, not a menu offering short-payment options
that are attractive to the supplier but only qualify at unobserved cent prices.

  Opening: EUR 4.60, 60 days, 36 months, AS-14404/45%/5%/2026-08.
    Cite the verified June/September PO history. Ask for a binding complete
    counteroffer. Do not assert the supplier already accepted these terms.
  First open counter: recompute every quoted package; accept any eligible
    qualifying one first. Otherwise propose EUR 5.00 on the same structure,
    citing the fresh PPI decline. Resolve an explicit structural objection now.
  Next open counter: accept a qualifying offer first. Otherwise make the
    EUR 5.10 closing proposal on the same structure with the unused AS decline.
    This is the highest nominal price authorised. Use the evidence once.
  Any final offer: accept the exact eligible package if it qualifies; otherwise
    stop under the existing strict policy. Do not reserve essential terms for
    a later turn or send another approval/clarification loop.

Finality can occur after the second proposal; a third evidence turn is not
guaranteed. If this test ends early or 60 days is refused, record that result
instead of pretending another fresh argument guarantees more discussion.
At 14/30/45 days, under the observed quoting pattern, the target would need to
be EUR 5.00 or less. Do not convert a EUR 5.10 short-payment quote into a deal.

The existing V21 controller still sends its A/B/C menu and then the automatic
EUR 5.09/45-day compromise. A prompt-only change would conflict with this test.
A future implementation must replace those controls for this experimental
variant while retaining acceptance validation and final-offer stopping. The
analysis/profile files here are not automatically loaded into live decisions.

Suggested internal state for that implementation
  evidence: source refs; actually sent; server check result; supplier's stated
    pricing response; observed package changes; no inferred discount credit.
  terms: complete standing offer; alternatives and eligibility; requested
    structure; explicit restrictions; never mix prices and payment from options.
  authority: unknown / confirmed / explicitly limited; source message.
  stance: open / final / terminal, independent of courteous tone.
  economics: current full cost; threshold; qualifying permitted trades.

Decision order: exact qualifying acceptance -> terminal/final handling ->
explicit objection -> fresh relevant evidence plus one complete counteroffer.
All historical observations remain fallible context, not hidden supplier rules.
